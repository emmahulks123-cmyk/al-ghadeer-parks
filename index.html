The premise

Sold on a number that never arrives.

Seven to ten percent. It is the yield on almost every brochure here, and it is gross. Quoted before service charges, before management, before a single week of vacancy.

What reaches your account is closer to four to six. That gap is not a scandal. It is arithmetic nobody selling you a unit is paid to perform.

Better you hear it from us at the start than find it in year two.

What you receive

Every recommendation shows its working.

01Net yield model

Gross rent, the building's actual service charge rate, management, vacancy, and the transfer fee, laid out year by year rather than flattened into one percentage.

02Supply pipeline

What else completes within eighteen months of your handover, and what that volume tends to do to achievable rent. The analysis most often skipped.

03Developer record

Past handover dates against promised ones, escrow standing, and build quality on delivered stock. Track record is the most reliable predictor in off plan.

04Residency position

Whether the purchase clears the AED 2 million Golden Visa threshold, and precisely when. Off plan and ready property behave very differently here.

05Exit assessment

Resale liquidity in the community, typical days on market, and the buyer profile you would be selling into. Entry is easy here. Exit is worth planning.

06The case against

A written argument for not proceeding. If we cannot make one, that is itself a signal. Usually we can, and it is the page clients read twice.

Straight answers

The questions clients actually ask.

As a gross figure, often yes. As money in your account, no. Service charges in prime buildings run high, management takes its share, and no property is occupied fifty two weeks a year. Net yields in the communities we work in typically land between four and six percent. Any adviser quoting you a net figure above that should be asked to show the deductions.
It depends on how the purchase is registered. With a DLD registered developer, a twenty percent payment plus the transfer fee, and Oqood registration reflecting the full value, an application can usually proceed at that point. Structured differently, you may wait until completion, which can be two or three years. This is paperwork, not luck, and it is worth settling before you sign.
Delivery history first, measured as promised handover dates against actual ones across completed projects. Then escrow standing, build quality on delivered stock, and how they behaved in previous soft markets. Established names with long records carry less risk than a developer with one launch and a good render. We will tell you where a project sits on that spectrum.
In specific communities, yes. When several developers complete into the same district within a twelve to eighteen month window, rents soften exactly as your unit comes online, and a yield modelled at eight percent can arrive at five. This is measurable in advance. We map the completion pipeline around any property before it reaches your shortlist.
We are engaged by the buyer, and we disclose every fee in writing before work begins. We do not take developer incentives to steer you toward particular stock. It is a smaller business built this way, and it is the only structure under which our advice is worth anything to you.
Most of our clients are. We handle the process remotely as standard, including power of attorney arrangements, registration, and handover inspection. Many complete a purchase before they visit. We would still encourage a visit, though we recognise it is not always practical.
We are a small team and take a limited number of files, so we are candid about fit. Most engagements begin around AED 2 million, which is also where the residency threshold sits. If your position is better served elsewhere, we will say so in the first conversation rather than the fifth.

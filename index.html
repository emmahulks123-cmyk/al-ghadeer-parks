The Premise

Most property here is sold on a number that never arrives.

Seven to ten percent. It is the yield on almost every brochure in this market, and it is a gross figure. It is quoted before service charges, before management, before a single week of vacancy, and before the transfer fee.

What actually reaches your account is closer to four to six. That gap is not a scandal. It is simply arithmetic that nobody selling you a unit is paid to perform.

We would rather you heard it from us at the start than found it in year two.

The Process

Three steps. No viewings until the third.

01

The brief

One conversation about capital, horizon, residency intentions, and genuine appetite for risk. We do not show you property in this meeting. Understanding what you are solving for comes first, and it saves months later.

02

The shortlist

Three to five options, rarely more. Each arrives with the net figure worked through, the supply picture for that specific community, the developer's delivery record, and where the Golden Visa threshold actually falls on your timeline.

03

The decision

We view, negotiate, structure, and handle registration. If nothing on the list holds up under the final look, we tell you plainly and we wait. A year of patience has cost our clients far less than a wrong entry.

What You Receive

Every recommendation arrives with its working shown.

No property reaches your shortlist without these six pieces of work behind it. You keep the file whether you buy through us or not.

01Net yield model

Gross rent, service charges at the building's actual rate, management, vacancy, and the transfer fee, laid out year by year rather than summarised into one flattering percentage.

02Community supply pipeline

What else completes within eighteen months of your handover, and what that volume tends to do to achievable rent. This is the analysis most often skipped.

03Developer delivery record

Past handover dates against promised dates, escrow standing, and build quality on delivered stock. Track record is the single most reliable predictor in off plan.

04Residency position

Whether the purchase clears the AED 2 million Golden Visa threshold, and precisely when it does. Off plan and ready property behave very differently here.

05Exit assessment

Historic resale liquidity in the community, typical days on market, and the buyer profile you would be selling into. Entry is easy in this market. Exit is the part worth planning.

06The case against

A written argument for not proceeding. If we cannot make one, that is itself a useful signal. Usually we can, and it is the page clients read twice.

As a gross figure, often yes. As money in your account, no. Service charges in prime buildings run high, management takes its share, and no property is occupied fifty two weeks a year. Net yields in the communities we work in typically land between four and six percent. Any adviser quoting you a net figure above that should be asked to show the deductions.
It depends entirely on how the purchase is registered. With a DLD registered developer, a twenty percent payment plus the transfer fee, and Oqood registration reflecting the full value, an application can usually proceed at that point. Structured differently, you may wait until completion, which can be two or three years. This is a question of paperwork, not luck, and it is worth settling before you sign anything.
Delivery history first, measured as promised handover dates against actual ones across their completed projects. Then escrow standing, build quality on delivered stock, and how they have behaved in previous soft markets. Established names with long records carry less risk than a developer with one launch and a good render. We will tell you where a project sits on that spectrum.
